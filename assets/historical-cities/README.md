# Tang city plans · circa 750

**Generalized archaeological reconstructions** of Chang’an and Luoyang, integrated into the main atlas map. The detail appears automatically at city scale during **742–755 inclusive** and disappears outside that interval or when zooming out. The Tianbao years are a viewing interval for a circa-750 composite plan, not a claim that every feature stayed unchanged throughout that interval. Ward and gate names follow familiar plan/site names where identified, rather than a complete year-by-year name history.

## Build and use

Run `node scripts/build_historical_cities.cjs`, then `node scripts/sync_version.js`. Serve the repository and use **Layers → Chang’an / Luoyang city detail**, or set the timeline to 742–755 and zoom naturally into either city. The checked **Tang city detail (742–755)** layer can be turned off independently of the regional layers. The generator requires only Node.js and the repository's existing Chinese conversion helper. It reads its own [control points](control-points.json); neither generation nor rendering requires the pending iOS PR, an `ios/` directory, or `assets/tang_focus.json`.

Outputs are `cities.geojson`, `labels.geojson` and `catalog.json`. Geometry is RFC 7946, WGS84 longitude/latitude. Five decimal places compactly encode the reconstruction; they are **not positional accuracy**. Features have stable IDs, `city`, `kind`, bilingual/traditional titles, `from`, `until`, `referenceYear`, `sourceIDs`, `evidence` and `uncertaintyM`. Reviewed additions also include a publication/page/figure `sourceLocator`, shown in the inspector. Geometry and labels use separate identifiers; presentation panels and captions are not exported as historical features.

`js/city_plans.js` renders the data through the atlas's existing Mercator projection, SVG and camera, with no additional map library, tile service or font bundle. The three data files load only near the two capitals at close zoom during the viewing interval; ordinary national views do not fetch them. Labels stay at readable screen sizes and become more detailed as the reader zooms in. Regional markers and coarse water/administrative graphics yield to the local detail at street scale, with checkbox states preserved. A feature's source inspector and the city badge expose evidence, legend, uncertainty and data downloads. Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese and English use the main atlas language control.

The camera supports up to 1500× zoom near the two capitals during 742–755, including wheel, keyboard and pinch. High zoom uses more precise SVG view boxes, metric scale labels and restorable `zoom`/`center`/`aspect` URL parameters. Changing to an uncovered year hides city geometry, labels and its source inspector without resetting the camera. The small `historical-cities.html` compatibility page forwards prototype bookmarks to the main map. Native export and runtime changes remain a separate follow-up.

## Georeferencing and uncertainty

- **Chang’an:** metric grid rotated 2° east of true north. The approximate Taiji Palace point is assigned local `(0, 8030 m)`. Outer dimensions are 9,721 × 8,651.7 m; palace/imperial precinct dimensions use published measurements. Daming's generalized closure now follows the reported 1,674 m southern width, 1,135 m northern width and 2,256 m western depth. Ward modules and other envelopes remain rounded. Allow roughly **400 m**, or **500 m** for added nodes and canal sections; peripheral errors can be larger.
- **Luoyang:** the approximate Dingding and Yingtian Gate points set direction and scale, assigning Yingtian `(0, 4900 m)`. Selected southern/northeastern ward modules and precinct envelopes are rounded reconstructions of published layout relationships. Allow roughly **350 m**, or **500 m** for river corridors and the northern palace sub-area. Neither modern roads nor a present-day river centerline is used as ancient geometry.
- These are editorial placement allowances, **not statistical confidence bounds**. `schematic` means a generalized reconstruction; `approximate-site` means a site point. Small hall/temple sites are points, not inferred building outlines. Dashed edges and line widths are map symbols, not surveyed walls, roads or river banks.

## Coverage and actual gaps

**Chang’an:** outer enclosure and gates; Taiji palace city with eastern/Yeting/warehouse sub-areas; imperial precinct; generalized Daming/Xingqing precincts; West Inner Garden; main avenues, selected named wards, East/West Markets and their attested internal street arrangement; additional palace gates and hall/temple sites; Taiye Pool; three **short known canal sections**. Qujiang and Furong are area points, with no invented shore or garden perimeter. Full canal connections, ward-internal lanes, houses and individual palace footprints remain omitted. Hanguang Hall (781), the Taiye/Penglai Pool perimeter galleries (817) and the Qujiang banks surveyed for the 827–840 phase are not projected back into 750.

**Luoyang:** north/east/south walls, outer/palace gates, palace/imperial/east/granary precincts and an approximate northern palace sub-area; Dingding/Duanmen axis and Tianjin crossing; six selected southern ward rows and selected northeastern wards; South/North Markets; schematic Luo/Chan corridors. Irregular western walls, exact river banks/islands, unresolved ward-edge cells, disputed market phases, Shangyang Palace boundaries and individual palace buildings remain omitted. Modern roads, railway lines and villages depicted as orientation aids in Su Bai fig. 6 are excluded.

Focused validation covers deterministic output, valid geometry/IDs/source references, viewing boundaries, automatic zoom/pan visibility, lazy loading, layer toggles, browser feature/source inspection, restorable views, three languages, desktop/phone visuals and the existing atlas validation script. No new application test suite, native build or performance profile is added by this web-only PR.
